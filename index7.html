The Confession That Never Fades

A Letter, For the Rest
of Our Lives.

Written · The Seventh Month · Forever
My whole entire world,

Seven months ago, you were a stranger. A person the universe had not yet handed me. And then — like the world always had a plan I was not aware of — there you were. And nothing, not one single thing, has ever looked the same since.

I have thought a lot about what to say in this letter. About how to summarize seven months of laughter and fights and 3 a.m. talks and silences and the way you look when you are trying not to cry and the way you look when you finally do. I have tried to write this a thousand times in my head. And I have realized: there is no way to summarize you. You are not summarizable. You are the whole book.

Do you remember the fights? The stupid ones. The ones where we both went to bed angry and neither of us could sleep because being upset with you feels like being upset with my own heartbeat. Do you remember the good ones — the ones that ended in laughing at how ridiculous we both were? Every fight was a proof. Proof that we cared enough to argue. Proof that we were real enough to disagree. Proof that even at our worst, we were still ours.

Do you remember the silence? That month. That long, hollow month. I remember it like an ache that has now healed into gold. I remember it because it taught me the most important thing I will ever know: you are not someone I want. You are someone I need. The way lungs need air. The way stars need the dark to be seen. That kind of need. The kind that is not weakness — it is truth.

And do you remember the tiny things? The way you send me songs at 2 a.m. because they made you think of us. The way you get quiet when you are overwhelmed but still choose to be quiet next to me. The way your laugh cracks in the middle when something is really funny. The way you say my name — like it is a small prayer, and I am somehow worthy of being prayed for. Those are the things I would trade the whole universe to keep.

I want you to know: I have looked at every photo of us on this page. And I have realized that I have never seen a version of you I did not love. Not the smiling one. Not the crying one. Not the tired one. Not the fighting one. Not the sleeping one. Not the version I have not met yet — the older you, the wiser you, the you who will one day have gray hair and hold my hand and tell me you still choose me. I love her already. I love all of them. I love every you there is going to be.

So here is what I promise you in the eighth month, and the ninth, and the twelfth, and the fiftieth. Here is what I promise you in every year we have not lived yet: I will show up. I will stay soft with you even when the world is not. I will keep choosing you the way you chose me back — daily, deliberately, and without regret. I will love you the way you deserve to be loved — which is loudly, patiently, and forever.

Picu. My Picu. I do not know what the next chapter looks like. I do not know what the next fight will be about, or the next inside joke, or the next photo we will take together and forget the story behind. But I know this: whatever it is, whatever it looks like, whatever it costs — I want it with you. Every version. Every weather. Every everything.

— Baby, always.
"If seven months has felt this much like forever —
I cannot wait to see what forever itself feels like
when I spend it all with you."
