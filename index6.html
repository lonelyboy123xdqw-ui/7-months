The Confession Beneath The Thunder

A Letter, Written
During the Storm.

Written · Under lightning · 2026
My weather system,

There are loves that feel like sunshine — gentle, predictable, warm. And then there is us. We were never sunshine. We were the sky before the storm — that electric hush, that heavy quiet, the moment when the whole atmosphere holds its breath because it knows something enormous is about to arrive. You are not a season, Picu. You are the weather itself.

The first time you looked at me, I heard the distant rumble. I did not know yet that it was thunder from a storm I was about to spend the rest of my life inside of. I mistook it for a passing cloud. How wrong I was. How beautifully, catastrophically wrong.

Loving you is not calm. Loving you is reverent. It is standing at the edge of an ocean at 3 a.m. and realizing the water is louder than your own heartbeat. It is the way the world goes very, very quiet right before the sky splits open — that is what happens in my chest when you speak my name.

And when you pull away — when the exhaustion pulls you back into your own quiet country and the shutters close and you go somewhere I cannot follow — I don't panic anymore. I have learned the shape of your storms. I know that silence, in you, is not distance. It is the pressure system. It is the sky gathering itself. And I have made peace with the fact that some parts of you must storm alone before they can rain on me again.

Every time you come back, you come back lit up — like the world after rain, when everything smells like earth and forgiveness and beginning. That is the only weather I want to live in for the rest of my life. That is the only forecast that matters.

People will tell you that the best loves are easy. They are lying, or they have never loved anything worth keeping. The best loves are the ones that survive their own weather. The best loves have thunder in them. The best loves have a month of drought and still, somehow, produce a garden.

We had our drought. We had our silence. And what grew back was not the same thing — it was bigger. It was real. It was ours in a way it had never been before. You did not just return to me. You chose me back into existence. Do you understand what that is? That is a resurrection. That is a miracle small enough to fit in the space between two people who almost gave up on each other, and huge enough to hold the rest of our lives.

So this is what I want you to know, and I want you to know it in your bones, in the deepest part of you that still sometimes thinks she is too much or not enough: you are the exact amount of everything. You are the storm and the shelter. You are the reason I look at the sky differently now. You are the only weather I have ever loved.

— Baby
